AssetTrack — How I approached it

Requirements, workflows, validation, and future roadmap

›

The Problem

IT assets are often tracked through spreadsheets and disconnected systems. That makes it harder to see where equipment is, who has it, what needs maintenance, whether warranties are expiring, and how much the hardware is worth.

My Approach

I defined business requirements around inventory, employees, assignments, maintenance, warranties, QR labeling, depreciation, reporting, and operational analytics. I then organized those workflows into a centralized application architecture.

What I Built

AssetTrack connects hardware inventory, employee equipment, repair tickets, financial reporting, warranty management, QR asset labels, audits, backup/restore, and analytics through shared application state.

How I Validated It

I reviewed and refined AI-assisted code, tested functionality across modules, debugged issues, worked through realistic workflows, organized sample data, and checked that updates remained synchronized across the system.

What I’d Expand Next

Authentication, a cloud database, role-based access control, audit logs, notifications, multi-device synchronization, REST APIs, and a mobile experience.

What It Shows

Business requirements, systems thinking, workflow modeling, testing, technical troubleshooting, documentation, and planning beyond the first release.

02

Blueprint — How I approached it

Product thinking, AI, architecture, and roadmap

›

The Problem

Job seekers can find thousands of openings but still struggle to understand which roles fit them, which skills they are missing, whether their resume is competitive, and what they should improve next.

Why It Mattered to Me

Blueprint came from something I experienced myself while transitioning from college into the job market. I wanted something that went beyond showing jobs and actually helped people understand how prepared they were for them.

My Approach

I defined the core user questions, planned the platform foundation, structured future capabilities around career recommendations and skills analysis, and designed the architecture so AI requests could be handled securely through the backend.

What I Built

A full-stack foundation using React, TypeScript, Node, Express, and Google Gemini. The platform is being developed around career recommendations, skill-gap analysis, resume improvement, job discovery, and personalized guidance.

Decisions I Made

I focused on scalability, security, maintainability, and user experience. Sensitive API credentials remain server-side, the interface uses reusable components, and the roadmap is organized around features that build toward a larger career ecosystem.

What I’d Expand Next

Career readiness scoring, application tracking, resume optimization, interview simulation, salary insights, portfolio analysis, user accounts, notifications, and a personalized career roadmap.

React TypeScript Vite Tailwind CSS Node.js Express Google Gemini
03

StudyFlow — How I approached it

Workflow consolidation, modular design, and AI assistance

›

The Problem

Students often manage their academic life across several disconnected tools — course portals, calendars, notes, assignment trackers, timers, and AI applications.

My Approach

I organized those disconnected activities into one student workflow: courses, assignments, study planning, exams, focus sessions, analytics, and AI-supported learning.

What I Built

StudyFlow combines course management, assignment tracking, a study planner, exam center, Pomodoro timer, productivity analytics, backup/restore, dark mode, and an AI study assistant.

Architecture

The application uses modular React components and shared state while an Express backend handles system telemetry and Google Gemini requests. Local storage and JSON backup/restore support persistence.

What It Taught Me

The project strengthened my experience with full-stack development, modular system design, API integration, organizing user workflows, and designing around a real-world productivity problem.

What I’d Expand Next

Canvas integration, Google Calendar sync, authentication, cloud persistence, multi-device support, and a mobile application.
